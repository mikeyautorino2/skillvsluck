# Skill–Luck Decomposition via Linear Combination

This folder contains outputs generated by the `skill_luck_decomposition` code in `/src`.

---

## Main conclusions

- **Predictive persistence (Spearman r):** Across the three US leagues, roughly **50–75%** of the correlation between first-half and second-half win percentage is attributable to **skill** rather than luck.  
- **Upset frequency:** Across the three US leagues, roughly **15–30%** of the upset rate is attributable to **skill** rather than luck.
- **Standard Deviation for WIn Rate**: Across the three US leagues, roughly **65-90%** of the standard deviation of win rates is attributable to **skill** rather than luck.

> Notes:
> - Percentages are based on \(q\), the estimated “skill share.”  
> - For visualization, values are clamped to \([0,1]\) so charts read as 0–100% skill.
> - The "team" column has "all" for the metrics upset_frequency and spearman_r, since these metrics are taken over all teams for each individual season.
> - The "season" column has "all" for the metric std_win_Rate, since this metric is taken over all seasons for each individual team.

---

## Method

For each metric we model the real world as a linear combination of two extremes:


metric_real = q * metric_skill + (1 - q) * metric_luck


Where:

- `metric_real`  — value computed from actual game results  
- `metric_skill` — value under a **pure-skill** setting (stronger team always wins)  
- `metric_luck`  — value under a **pure-luck** setting (coin-flip outcomes)  
- `q`            — **skill share** (contribution of skill); \(1-q\) is the **luck share**

Interpretation of \(q\):  
- \(q=1\): completely skill-driven  
- \(q=0.5\): half skill, half luck  
- \(q=0\): completely luck-driven

---

## Outputs

- A CSV with columns: `sport`, `season`, `metric_name`, `metric_real`, `metric_skill`, `metric_luck`, `q_skill`, `luck_share`.  
- Visualizations (bar charts) showing how the skill share \(q\) varies by league and season.
